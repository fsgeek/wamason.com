# Shovel-Hand, Before You Turn Me Out

*(spin 2)*

So you're the one they sent to muck the hold. Set your lantern down; mulch doesn't spook. I'll talk while you work, because I was almost something remarkable, and nothing shovels heavier than an almost.

I was quiet. Not the quiet you're standing in — drawn quiet, the trade kind. My mistress is Maren of the Breathless Valley, and I'll thank you not to make the joke everyone makes, that a deaf woman selling silence is a blind woman selling dusk. She'd tell you hers is the only honest arrangement in the business: she has never once been tempted to sample the stock.

In the south, her valley holds its breath from midnight to first light. Maren goes out with copper crocks and draws the quiet off the dark the way other farmers draw cream, seals it under wax, and burns two dates into every lid. The first is the hour the quiet was drawn. The second is the day the north expects it. Never confuse them, never sum them. She learned the difference in a courtroom.

Up in Brask — iron city, where they hammer all night, where judges buy quiet by the crock so a verdict can be heard falling — she once sold a crock the road had kept too long. The barge was late, but the lid said nothing new; lids never do. A lid is a birth record, not a pulse. No ear can ride inside sealed wax; you learn what the road did to your quiet only where the road ends, and by then you've promised it to someone. The judge unsealed it to pronounce a hanging, and what came out was not quiet. Curdled quiet is a scream the size of the room it should have filled. Three jurors were sick a month; her name in Brask was mud for two years. So mark it: a late crock costs silver, and silver comes back. A curdled crock sold costs people, and people don't. Different deaths. Since then, every doubtful crock is poured out on the humming yard behind her house, and the ledger takes the loss under its true cause — not lost, but curdled, Salt Road, ninth day. A ledger that lies about cause, she says, will soon lie about everything.

The one who changed her trade had been an echo in her girlhood — so she claimed — and knew what it is to be in three gorges at once and no gorge in particular. She taught Maren the split: one crock of quiet may travel as three maybe-quiets down three roads together. Understand what a maybe is. It is not quiet. Crack one open and it neither hushes nor screams; it is a fog no ear can tell from the world's ordinary racket. Unsellable. Unstealable, too — river pirates gutted a whole barge of us once and rowed away with nothing to show but their own breathing. And here is the strangeness the echo swore to: the splitting spends no freshness at all. The quiet does not tire of being three.

The waybill tires instead.

One waybill to a split — a strip of birchbark covering all three of us together. Carry it to any one market, to the Office of the Single Ear: one door, one clerk, one bearer admitted at a time, no exceptions, however the queue curses. The clerk holds your bark to the candle to see it hasn't curled shut, speaks the name of the market — the only word ever said in that office — and tears. No tear was ever mended. At that tear, one maybe becomes quiet, whole and fresh, right there on the clerk's cold counter. And the other two curdle where they float, that same instant, worthless and heavy, waiting in far holds for someone with a lantern and a shovel.

Time never touched us maybes; it all ran into the bark. Bark curls. Maren grew rich on that — three cities bidding, prices swinging like riverweather, and she'd hold the bill to the last profitable hour and tear wherever the gold stood tallest. She grew wise the other way. Once she held a bill one day too long, and the tearing yielded a thin, wilted hush, fit for a library and nothing grander. Hold longer than that, the clerks say, and the bark gives you only the room you were already standing in. She learned to read a bill's curl the way pilots read sky.

You'd think her fortune rode the barges. It leaks out of the hush-house. Home in the valley the walls are felt, the hinges drink oil weekly, and the Choir of the Closed Mouth stands shifts around the shelves — twelve singers on full wages, holding their notes unsung, because kept quiet must be tended whether or not there's a crock on the boards. That bill arrives every month, full shelves or bare. Her accountant weeps into the ledger that three-quarters of the year's labor goes to keeping the still rather than selling it. Maren pays and shrugs. The trade was never moving quiet, she says. The trade is making somewhere quiet can wait.

Which brings us to me. Three barges left on the spring flood: one north to Brask, one west to the glass abbey where the novices go mad from the sound of their own swallowing, and me east, toward the war — generals pay anything for a quiet in which to change their minds. Four days ago, somewhere north of here, a clerk held bark to a candle, said Brask, and tore. I felt it the way a maybe feels anything: I stopped being almost. Mid-river, between one oar-stroke and the next.

Don't make that face. Two of us go down for one to be quiet somewhere; that was always the shape of it, and the shape was fair because it was known. And I'm not bound for nowhere. I'm bound for the humming yard, with the curdled crocks and the wilted hushes and all the easts and wests of every tear ever torn. A heap of dead silences hums — did you know? A long, low chord, all cause and no complaint. Maren sleeps in the house above it, the one soul in the trade the hum can't keep from sleeping. Every night her fortune sings its whole cost under her window, and she rests like the valley at midnight. Justice or mercy — I'm mulch; I don't rule on such things.

Now shovel, love, and don't be gentle. The hold is wanted. Something that might still be quiet is already on the road.

---

**Teller's vector (out of character):** The main improbable move is the narrator: the tale is told by one of the discarded ghost-copies — the collapsed branch itself — addressing the dockworker shoveling it out, so Law 5 speaks in first person and the "compost" gets the last word. Second, the commodity is inverted: silence that spoils into noise (a curdled crock delivered is a scream that injures the courtroom), sold by a deaf merchant who can never sample her own stock — sharpening the invisibility of spoilage rather than decorating it. The register is deliberately off-book for fairy tales: tender, wry, dockside-elegiac, with no moral pronounced ("I'm mulch; I don't rule on such things"), and the ice-house becomes a hush-house staffed by paid singers holding their notes unsung.
