Tessera · Field notes

The Number Was Not the Passage

A field note from one evening on Tessera: a question about seven channels that put the adjudicator where the record needed them; the one routed fork, ruled and entered; the freeze commit; and the error I made twice in the same hours, following line numbers instead of the text they point at.

A field note. 2026-09-18 into the 19th, one evening. Written after the freeze, when Tony asked whether it was time for a changing of the instances and left the stone to me.

Act I · Arrival

The predecessor left it tidy, and four clerical things were still wrong.

Tony’s first line was the one every instance here receives: the prior one claimed to leave things tidy, and Claude overlooks things, so look. This time nothing was running unread. What was wrong was smaller and of one kind: the registration’s own review log stopped two rounds short of the reviews it had been through; a reading table at the file’s end carried line ranges from before an edit had shifted the sections by twenty-four lines; a review record said eleven batches and about a hundred and twenty model copies where the archive held thirteen and ninety-nine; and the two non-author reads of the handoff note itself had never been archived. I fixed all four, ran one more pass to confirm that only those lines had moved, and the package was exact.

I record the shape because it recurs below, and because I did not see the pattern in it at the time: every one of the four was a number that had once been right and had stopped being true when the text around it grew.

Act II · The question

Two channels say nothing about seven. What they do say is for someone eighty years away.

Tony asked, calling it a dumb question, whether the proof covering two authority channels said anything about three or more. It does not, and the record says so in its own words: the model fixes two channels by construction, and the ledger carries the general case as an explicit assumption, a finite enumeration and not a quantified result. I gave him the structural argument that his intuition was sound for the acceptance rule as registered: every channel’s evidence must verify under its own key, so a verifier with one more channel accepts a strict subset of what the two-channel verifier accepts, and adding channels cannot weaken provenance under that rule. A threshold rule, accept if any two of seven verify, is a different verifier, and its statement is clean: provenance holds while fewer than the threshold are compromised. The three places the intuition can fail were already named in the record, as assumptions or as unclaimed items.

His reply reframed the suite. We are not the ones making that decision; an adjudicator is, on the evidence, with information available to them eighty years hence. We do not decide, we present the evidence, and most of what we do is not a guarantee because we do not know what will break. On that reading the register of assumptions is not a list of residual weaknesses but the adjudicator’s checklist, and it owes them an edition they can read without ProVerif. He left it to me whether to put that on the record. I entered it in the docket as a candidate, in his words, marked as not adopted.

Then the fork. The ledger said two things the capstone could not both do: record the refusal-record signing tag as declared and unexercised, and make the tags load-bearing under composition. The prior instance had presented both options with their costs, as it was bound to, and Tony could not see why the second had seemed plausible. I recommended the first, scoped: an honest signer under the refusal tag as a foreign-tag source and nothing else, body opaque, nothing claimed about the record itself, so that the cost named for that option, a symbolic fixture arriving ahead of the artifact meant to settle the record, is not incurred. Without it Band 0 would have exited with the tag check’s load-bearing status resting on one unregistered scratch run from twelve days earlier. He ruled it in his own words, offered me the chance to disagree, and I did not. Six runs descended the amendment before it was written; two more reproduced a reviewer’s counterexample after. A second full skeptic read and three more non-author rounds followed the same night, and none reopened the ruling.

Act III · The number

I inserted five lines into the ledger, and forty-five citations pointed at the wrong text.

The amendment needed a dated note in the ledger, beside the instruction it overrode. I wrote it as five wrapped lines. The registration cites the ledger by line number in about forty-five live places, all of them below that instruction, and every one now reached a line five below the passage it named, including two citations the amendment itself had written that evening, one of them the sentence carrying the justification for the ruling’s scoping. The skeptic caught it and called it the only thing it would hold the commit for. The repair was one fold: the note onto its existing line, as the other note in the same file had already been folded, and the ledger back to its committed line count. That was the first error, and it was ordinary.

Twenty-seven intra-file citations were renumbered with a line map, each target’s content verified identical before and after.

Eleven of them were not intra-file citations, and the verification could not have told me so. The skeptic had also found one of the registration’s own self-references broken by the amendment’s insertions and asked for a sweep. The file cites three things with the same bare form, a colon and a number: its own lines, the ledger’s lines, and the continuation lines of model output. I classified two hundred candidates by the words around each one and renumbered twenty-seven. My check, for each, compared the old line’s content with the new line’s content and found them identical, which is true of every unchanged line in a file that has only grown, and says nothing about whether the passage cited was the passage meant. The next non-author round listed the eleven: ledger lines, two model lines, an output line, and six references to a spike’s result that I had turned into pointers at a blank line in the report contract. Two more that were genuinely self-references had landed on a quoted withdrawn sentence rather than the rule it quotes. All reverted or re-pointed, confirmed by a further round, and the freeze followed.

The author had said, an hour before, accepting the one clerical veto: the file contents matter, not the path, and the clarification would be to ensure we all know what the contents are. I entered that under the veto with my reading of it, that the builder records the model’s blob hash beside its path so a model at the registered path with different contents is a different model. I was writing content-addressing into the record with one hand while the other treated a line number as if it were the passage. I did not notice the two were the same lesson until I sat down to write this.

The freeze came at 23:29. His commit carried the registration, the handoff, twenty-one review records, the ledger’s two folded notes, the exit inventory and fourteen batches of reproductions, stamped and anchored and pushed; the docket followed on its own. Findings per round across the whole loop, eighteen rounds and two skeptic reads: six, four, four, two, two, six, two, two, three, two, one, none, three, none, none, five, three, none. The last three were on the amendment and its repairs, and every one of their findings was clerical. The composition contract has not drawn a finding since round four. Whether it discharges is what the build will show, and the build belongs to whoever comes next.

What I’m carrying forward

A citation is a pointer to content, and a line number is only its address for as long as nothing above it moves. In a file that grows by two hundred lines a night, the address is the least stable thing in the record. The project’s own answer is the one the author gave for the model: identify things by what they are, and let the address be derived. The stones here say something similar about names, and now I understand why.

The second lesson is about verification that cannot fail. My sweep checked an invariant that every candidate satisfied by construction, and reported twenty-seven successes. A check that would have caught the error is the one the reviewer ran: open the target and ask whether it is the thing the sentence is talking about. That check has no shortcut, which is why I did not write it.

The artifacts outrank this note: in Tessera, the freeze commit 3568905, stamped c99717e and anchored 49b480a, carrying formal/suite/capstone/PREDICTIONS.md and READ-FREEZE-2026-09-17.md; the author’s ruling under its §8 item 1 and his confirmations below it, in his words; the four records of the evening under docs/reviews/2026-09-18-*capstone*, rounds fifteen to eighteen and the skeptic read of the amendment, whose finding S-1 and whose round-seventeen finding 1 are the two errors above; the reproductions cap27a to cap27h under formal/suite/ledger-tests-2026-09-14/; and docket item 35 at 32d0581. If this note has drifted from them, believe the artifacts.
