Nearly four decades in systems software, from operating-system kernels and distributed file systems to the reliability of large language models.

Tony Mason, who appears in patents, court records, and books as W. Anthony Mason, has worked in systems software since the late 1980s. He began in operating-system and distributed-systems research at Stanford, on David Cheriton's V system, then spent four years at Transarc building distributed file systems: AFS, DCE/DFS, and Episode, the last of which still runs today as the POSIX file system of IBM's z/OS, more than thirty years on. From 1994 to 2016 he was a Consulting Partner at OSR Open Systems Resources, leading Windows file system and filter-driver architecture for more than 160 technology companies; the twelve US patents, two of the three books, and the still-shipping File Encryption Solution Framework date from that period. Since 2016 he has worked independently through wamason.com LLC as a consultant and expert witness. From 2017 to 2025 he returned to graduate study, completing a PhD at the University of British Columbia under Margo Seltzer and Ada Gavrilovska; his dissertation, Indaleko, built a unified personal index across heterogeneous storage. His current research concerns the reliability of large language model systems.

  • Microsoft Research, Cambridge UK 2020
    Visiting Researcher (fixed-term) · Cambridge, UK
    Built a discrete-event simulation of holographic storage media wear; work contributed to "Holographic Storage for the Cloud" (ACM Transactions on Storage, 2025).
  • OSR Open Systems Resources 1994–2016
    Consulting Partner; Vice-President & Treasurer · Amherst, NH
    Led Windows file system filter-driver architecture and delivery for 160+ technology companies, including IBM, Microsoft, Cisco, Amazon, HP, Dell, Intel, EMC, NetApp, Seagate, and Symantec.
    Designed the File Encryption Solution Framework (FESF) and the isolation-filter architecture for transparent file encryption, compression, and streaming data delivery, still shipping in 2026 and a precursor to container/cloud storage virtualization patterns.
    Designed the FSDK filter driver that underpinned Microsoft App-V for more than a decade before Microsoft reimplemented the pattern across its cloud, container, and version-control workflows.
  • Transarc Corporation 1989–1993
    Member of Technical Staff (later Area Manager) · Pittsburgh, PA
    Built distributed file systems on unreliable hardware, AFS, DCE/DFS, and the Episode file system, which remains in production today as the POSIX file system of IBM's z/OS platform 35+ years later.
  • Stanford University 1987–1989
    V Operating System / Distributed Systems Group · Stanford, CA
    Kernel and distributed-systems work on the V Operating System in David Cheriton's Distributed Systems Group, an early microkernel and distributed OS research platform.
